Discover Nuweiba

Where the Desert Meets the Red Sea

The Setting

Where Stillness, Story, and Strategy Meet

On the eastern edge of the Sinai Peninsula lies Nuweiba—a place where mountains meet the Red Sea. Unlike the resorts of Sharm or the bustle of Dahab, Nuweiba remains understated and quietly powerful. It is a meeting point of desert and sea, history and presence, patience and play.

Dramatic granite mountains frame a wide coastal plain, glowing red and gold at sunrise. Coral reefs begin meters from shore, where turtles, rays, and dolphins are frequent visitors. Life here moves at a pace dictated by wind, tide, and temperature.

Tawla: The Game of the Region

Backgammon, known locally as tawla, has been played across this region for thousands of years. In cafés throughout Egypt and the Middle East, the clack of dice on wood is as familiar as the call to prayer. Tawla is a social ritual, a contest of foresight and nerve, a quiet duel played in public.

Like the landscape itself, the game balances chance and control. Dice introduce uncertainty; strategy determines outcome. Victory comes not from force, but from timing, restraint, and reading both board and opponent.

In Nuweiba, tawla feels particularly at home. The game mirrors the environment: long stretches of calm punctuated by sudden shifts; patience rewarded, haste punished. Played slowly, often in silence broken only by the sea and the dice, it becomes almost meditative.

A Place of Alignment

Nuweiba reveals itself gradually to those willing to sit still. Days are marked by swims over coral gardens, simple meals on the sand, and conversations without agenda. Nights bring vast skies and a sense of scale that recalibrates perspective.

Whether contemplating ancient stories, watching light shift across the Gulf of Aqaba, or considering your next move over a backgammon board, the lesson is the same: you play the hand you're given, but how you play it matters.

Big Dune Camp

Your tournament home. Big Dune Camp is a notable beach camp nestled directly between the mountains of Sinai and the Red Sea along the Gulf of Aqaba in Nuweiba (the Duna / The Dune area). Known for its wide stretch of white sandy beach, proximity to Sinai's iconic desert sand dunes, and authentic Bedouin hospitality.

Camp management keeps the place clean and serves delicious local dishes.

Explore the desert and mountains on a camel safari — just steps from the camp.

Comfortable and well-kept camp rooms — a relaxing retreat after a day of play.

Your Accommodation

Accommodation in Nuweiba is refreshingly simple yet deeply special. The rooms all directly on the beach, so the Red Sea is never out of sight or sound. Rooms are private bungalows or chalets, each with air conditioning for comfort during the warmer months, but the real luxury is waking up to the sea itself. You open your door straight onto sand, palm trees, and clear turquoise water, with the mountains of Sinai rising behind you. There is no concrete skyline, no rush, and no noise beyond the wind, the waves, and the occasional call to prayer drifting across the bay. It is the kind of place where time slows down, mornings start with barefoot walks along the shore, and evenings end under star-filled skies.

  • ✓
    Traditional Huts (Husha): Simple, bamboo or palm-frond huts right on the sand with shared bathroom facilities — ideal for budget travelers and backpackers.
  • ✓
    Private Bungalows & Chalets: Air-conditioned brick or wooden chalets with private bathrooms and sea or mountain view balconies.
  • ✓
    Bedouin Dining & Facilities: Open-air Bedouin restaurant serving local dishes & fresh seafood, 24/7 electricity in upgraded units, basic Wi-Fi, and free private parking.
  • ✓
    Beach & Leisure: Wide private beach with shaded sitting, hammocks, bean bags, sun loungers, and easy shore-entry snorkeling into clear Red Sea coral reefs.
  • ✓
    Excursions & Nights: Evening Bedouin bonfires & stargazing, plus day trips to Colored Canyon, White Canyon, Mt. Sinai & St. Catherine's Monastery.
